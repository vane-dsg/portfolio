    Photo booth for a design event

    I created an AI-powered photo booth experience, from the visual concept to a working product, in a single day and without writing a line of code.

    • Design with AI
    • Volunteer project
    My role
    Idea, flow, UX/UI and directing the development with AI
    Tools
    Figma, Claude, VS Code, Supabase and GitHub Pages
    Project type
    Volunteer work

    Goal

    I went to an event held in the same venue where our community event would take place a week later. There was a single-person study booth there, and we thought: why not use this space to make a photo booth for our event?

    1. 01

      Give attendees a different and fun way to capture the event.

    2. 02

      Every story posted would carry the event's visual identity and @uxfor.br.

    Challenges

    • Quick to use, no instructions

      People walk in, take their photos and leave. The app had to be easy and intuitive.

    • Sending to the phone

      The photos had to leave the laptop and reach each person's phone, ready to post.

    • No budget

      A volunteer project, with no budget for paid hosting tools.

    Process

    01. I started with the problem

    Before any screen, I wrote to the AI as if it were a briefing: the setting (a laptop in a booth, during an event), who would use it, what happened step by step and what people took home at the end. With that context, the AI had less to guess and asked about what was missing.

    What I said

    i want to build an app for the following scenario:

    • i'll leave my laptop in a booth during an event so people can go in and take photos
    • when they enter the booth they click take photo
    • the screen should behave like a real photo booth
    1. a 3 second countdown shows up so the person can get ready for the photo
    2. this repeats 3 times (3 photos)
    3. after taking the 3 photos, the system shows a qr code on the screen that can be scanned with a phone
    4. when the qr code is opened on the phone, the photos show up inside the template i'll make (a template ready for instagram stories) and the separate photos too (individual photos)

    my questions before starting this project:

    1. how do i connect the system running on the web with the phone
    2. can i keep the app local only? or would it be better to host it somewhere? (i thought about github)

    What Claude answered

    Based on my prompt, it walked me through the whole solution architecture: the initial flow, how to connect the web booth to the phone through a QR code, hosting the app, storing the photos in the cloud and making them available, plus anticipating technical limits, risks and error states. All of that before writing any code.

    02. Design in Figma

    Based on the flow Claude gave me, I designed the waiting screen, the camera, the QR code screen and the phone page with the event's visual identity. Then I sent the frame links to the AI and asked it to build following that layout.

    03. Deciding how to build it

    I didn't know the tools, so for each decision I asked the AI to show me the options and what changed with each one. The final call was mine.

    DecisionChoiceWhy
    Where to write the codeA folder in VS Code, with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no frameworkA small site I could open and test on my own
    Where to store the photosSupabaseFree, with enough storage for the event.
    Where to publishGitHub PagesFree and with https, which the camera requires

    04. Development

    I connected the project folder to Claude, and it created and edited the files there. My job was a loop: ask, test in VS Code and come back saying what was right and what had to change. In Supabase, I sent a screenshot of each step whenever I got stuck. I learned what a bucket is, what an access rule is for and which key can stay in the code.

    05. Testing and bugs

    Nothing came out perfect the first time. For each bug, I told the AI what happened, where it happened and sent a screenshot.

    BugWhat happenedHow it was fixed
    Filter on SafariBlack and white photos were saved in colorThe filter started being applied to the image itself, pixel by pixel
    Grain on the phoneIn the camera preview, the filter moved around and was too strongThe effect was redone to stay still and lighter
    Broken imagesSome of the template drawings didn't show upThe SVG files were simplified

    06. Iterations

    During the event: photos from the phone

    I noticed some people might not go to the booth but would still want the template. So the site started working on phones too, and I spread QR codes to the site around the venue.

    After the event: photos from the gallery

    People wanted to keep using the template with photos they had already taken. So, before the camera, I added a screen with two options: take a photo or choose from the gallery. Choosing the gallery puts the selected photos into the story templates.

    Result

    50+

    Photo sessions at the event. Each session is someone who took (or chose) photos and got the story template ready, with the UXFor identity.

    What I learned

    1. 01

      AI writes the code, but the design decisions are still mine. I was the one who saw what to build, how each screen should be, when something was wrong and what to change after people used it.

    2. 02

      A good prompt looks like a briefing. Setting, who uses it, what happens and the expected result. The more context, the less the AI has to guess.

    3. 03

      Test and fix after every answer.

    4. 04

      The idea worked because it was a simple project. It was a small site, with no login and no payments. If there were sensitive data, someone who knows code would need to review what the AI wrote, especially for security.
