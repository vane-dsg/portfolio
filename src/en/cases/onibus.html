    Redesigning the Meu Ônibus public transit app

    A redesign focused on rethinking the whole experience to meet users' real needs.

    • Mobile
    • User research
    My role
    UX/UI Designer
    Type
    Personal project
    Timeline
    3 months

    What I did

    I ran the user research, created the persona and the journey map, and redesigned the app's flows and screens, from sketches to final screens.

    Context

    Meu Ônibus ("My Bus") is the public transit app of Fortaleza, built to bring together two everyday tasks for bus riders in one place.

    What the app does

    • Bus times at stops

      Shows in real time when each bus arrives at the stop.

    • Card top-up

      Tops up the transit card without going to a service point.

    Main problems

    • Usability issues

      Over time, the flows became confusing, the information poorly organized and the interface unintuitive.

    • Two apps

      The features were split: one app to check and top up the cards and another one just for bus times.

    This is an unofficial redesign, made as a study. It has no connection with the app or with the people who build it.

    Research

    I use the app every day, so I started with my own experience. Then I checked whether other users went through the same.

    Store reviews

    I analyzed the top reviews of both apps on the App Store and Play Store. I kept the ones that explained where users got stuck and discarded vague ones, like "the app is bad" or "it freezes too much".

    "When it opens, it could quickly show the bus stops and their times, but it takes several taps to see the information."

    1.0 · helpful to 317 people

    "Checking the balance is complicated, it takes several taps, and the font color doesn't contrast well with the background."

    1.0 · helpful to 159 people

    In-person observation

    5 people used the app's main features while I watched, following this script:

    1. What is it like to check the bus time?
    2. What is it like to check the transit card balance?
    3. What is it like to top up the card?

    In purple, the answers. In green, the ideas that came up.

    Persona

    I created the persona from the research, mainly from the in-person sessions. It helped me decide which problems to prioritize.

    Ana Carolina, 20 years old

    Advertising student in Fortaleza · lives far from downtown · takes the bus to college every day

    "It's so frustrating when the app says the bus is coming and it takes another 20 minutes! (...) Imagine getting on the bus and finding out there's no credit on your card!"

    Goals

    • get to and from college quickly, making the most of her time
    • have reliable times and routes to plan her trip
    • top up her card easily, without going to a service point

    Frustrations

    • needs several taps to see her card balance
    • has to leave the app and open another one to top up
    • has trusted the prediction before and the bus never showed up or took too long

    Behaviors

    • uses the app every day to check predictions and plan her trip
    • prefers apps that save time and simplify her routine

    Journey map

    What the user does, thinks and feels at each stage, and what could be better.

    Main problems

    After the whole research phase, I could define which pain points mattered most.

    1. 01.  Balance and top-up

      Checking the balance takes several taps, and the top-up flow is slow and confusing.

    2. 02.  Split apps

      To top up, the user has to leave Meu Ônibus and open another app.

    3. 03.  Usability and navigation

      Routes that are hard to find, a confusing interface and too many taps.

    4. 04.  Design and accessibility

      Low contrast and text in a color close to the background, hard to read for older people and people with low vision.

    Wireframes

    I sketched the new flows by hand before moving to Figma.

    1. 01.  Fewer taps in the old flow

      I revisited the old flow to shorten it and make tasks simpler.

    2. 02.  Balance and top-up first

      I prioritized checking the balance and topping up: seeing the balance took several taps, and topping up required another app.

    Result

    The before and after of each flow.

    01  Home

    Before
    • When you open the app, the map shows up far from the city. You have to zoom into the circle to see nearby stops.
    • Once zoomed in, there are so many markers that the map gets confusing.

    01  Home

    After

    The app opens right at the nearest stop, with the lines that stop there and how long until each bus arrives.

    The main card's balance sits at the top of the home screen, with zero taps.

    02  Search

    Before
    • Search fails if the right filter isn't chosen first. I searched for the line number with the "Local" filter selected and found nothing.
    • The search field disappears while typing.
    • After choosing a direction, you have to search everything again to switch.
    • Contrast is poor, and the map markers open the same screen under any filter.

    02  Search

    After
    • Lines, stops and trip became tabs of the same search, and each one shows suggestions before you type.
    • On the Trip tab, the user enters where they are and where they're going and sees the line combinations with the total time.

    02.1  Search results

    After
    • The line shows the full route, and you can switch direction on the same screen.
    • The stop shows every line that stops there and how long until each one arrives.
    • The trip shows the route on the map, the arrival time and where to get off.

    03  Balance and top-up

    Before
    • To top up, the user left Meu Ônibus and had to use another app.
    • Even in the top-up app, the balance was hidden among several buttons.
    • You had to choose the card before seeing the balance, one extra tap.
    • The balance, the most sought-after information, was at the bottom of the screen.

    03  Wallet

    After

    The wallet brings the transit cards together, each with its balance and a top-up button.

    The user picks the card they check most, and its balance shows up on the home screen.

    03.1  Top-up

    After

    The whole top-up flow on a single screen, inside Meu Ônibus: amount, payment method and confirmation.

    Auto top-up: the user saves a credit card, and the transit card is topped up automatically when the balance drops below one fare.

    Conclusion

    Listening to and watching other users was essential to propose solutions that hit the mark, before assuming anything.
